# Full-project rare-earth card corpus

This skill is the canonical output location for article, CIF structure, and review cards relative to this SKILL.md. The index is an extracted, fallible representation, not primary-source verification. Answer in the user's language.

## Retrieval and linkage

1. Start with `cluster_ledger.csv`, not a scan of all JSON files. From this skill directory run `python scripts/search_ledger.py --help` for exact `--doi`, `--compound-key`, `--ccdc`, `--refcode`, and element/reagent/family/shape/nuclearity or `--similar-to` queries. Search only compound rows first; inspect a small shortlist's original cards before answering.
2. `compound_key = DOI#compound[index]` identifies a card entry, not a globally stable chemical identity. `--reagent` covers any reagent role; `--ligand` searches only explicit ligand role tags. Empty means unknown. Similarity ranking offers candidates, not proven isostructurality.
3. DOI-level provenance is not a compound-level structure match. `linked_structure_*` fields contain resolved local links, still subject to source verification. Check `compound_structure_review.csv` for candidate matches; an ambiguous or missing local link is not evidence that no CIF exists. This file is a published warning snapshot, not an instruction to rerun production scripts. Review cards remain independent context.
4. In an article compound card, `ccdc_refcodes: ["LOCAL_..."]` is a **local CIF-card identifier** (the filename stem of `cif_structure_cards/LOCAL_....json`), not a CCDC-assigned refcode or deposition number. It marks a CIF obtained locally from the article/SI without a reported CCDC deposit in the card; `ccdc_numbers` may be empty and the CIF card may have `ccdc_number: null`. Match it to the same DOI and exact local structure-card `refcode`, then verify the compound assignment against the article/CIF. Do not search CSD for `LOCAL_...`, invent a CCDC number, or interpret a missing CCDC match as missing crystallographic data. Conversely, this marker alone does not prove that no CCDC entry exists elsewhere.

## Evidence discipline

- **Synthesis**: `extracted_cards/<DOI-normalized>.json` contains an article's `list_of_rare_earth_compounds`. Identify the *specific compound*, its `synthesis`, `material_class`, `properties`, `source_locations`, and whether SI was used. A paper-level condition must not be transferred to another compound in the same article. Preserve quantities, units, solvent, temperature, duration, pH, vessel, and workup as reported; absent/null fields mean *not available in this card*, not zero or ambient. Do not convert a reported route into a guaranteed reproducible protocol.
- **Analogous synthesis within one article**: If a target compound's `synthesis.procedure` says "prepared analogously to 1", "same method as 1 except ...", etc., do not stop at the abbreviated target entry or assume that the reference is always compound 1. First match the cited label **within the same article card** (`list_of_rare_earth_compounds[*].compound_label`), using formula/metal composition and source location to disambiguate labels such as `1`, `1a`, or solvates. Read the referenced compound's full `synthesis` fields and the target's own `synthesis` fields; follow a chain of references only when each step is unambiguous. For a requested expanded procedure, carry over only steps and quantities that the article explicitly makes shared, apply only explicitly stated changes (often, but not necessarily, a rare-earth or transition-metal salt), and keep target-specific workup/yield/crystallization separate. Show the base procedure, the source-reported substitution(s), and the resulting target-specific steps with **"shared from [label]" / "reported for [target]" / "inferred, verify"** tags. Check salt identity, hydration, concentration, stoichiometry and any changed solvent, temperature, pH, time or order; do not invent a replacement amount or claim equal moles/mass when not stated. If the base compound is missing, ambiguous, or itself lacks quantitative details (including SI not present in this corpus), give only the supported partial route and the missing information; never present an inferred route as a reported or validated recipe.
- **Structure**: `cif_structure_cards/<refcode>.json` includes `atom_sites_available`, `bond_table_available`, `metal_framework`, `candidate_structural_motifs`, `geometry_basis`, and `interpretation_limit`. If the matched CIF card is absent, answer available article-reported formula/structure description from the synthesis card **as article-level evidence**, but say that crystallographic parameters, independently checked connectivity, and topology are unavailable here; do not simply erase the article description. Distinguish article-reported formula/description from CIF-derived observations and algorithmic candidate motifs. If graph data are missing or symmetry bonds omitted, do not claim established connectivity, module assignment, molecular shape, oxidation states, or ligand roles from formula/site count alone. A metal–metal separation is not necessarily a bond. Nuclearity can be article-matched rather than independently derived from the CIF.
- **Review**: `review_knowledge_cards/<card_id>.json` contains `claim`, `scope`, `conditions`, `caveats`, `basis`, and `evidence_locations`. Apply a claim only inside its stated scope. Distinguish neutral beam clusters, coordination clusters, oxo clusters, and heterometallic clusters; do not universalize review trends or infer a specific synthetic condition from a general claim.
- **Cross-card reasoning**: label each sentence or bullet as directly reported, computed/derived, plausible inference, or unverified proposal when the distinction matters. For numerical comparisons, check definitions (rare-earth count vs total metals; formula unit vs discrete core), units, experimental conditions, and whether data come from the same measurement method. Highlight contradictions or encoding anomalies and verify against primary article/CIF before treating them as facts. Never fabricate a citation, an absent parameter, or a successful synthesis.

## Answer format

Give a direct answer, then a compact evidence table or bullets with **specific local file path + JSON field (and compound label/index)**, DOI or CCDC/refcode where available, followed by uncertainty and next verification. Cite review claims with `card_id` and DOI. For a speculative design request, provide an evidence-backed rationale and *hypotheses to test*, not a claim of validated chemistry; laboratory execution needs primary-source and safety review. If the local corpus cannot answer, say so and distinguish corpus absence from scientific impossibility. Browse primary sources when the user asks for current literature or authoritative verification; label that evidence separately from the local cards.

For task-oriented prompt patterns and an initial validation protocol, read `references/prompts.md`. Do not automatically run the evaluation for every answer.
